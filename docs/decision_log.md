# Author decisions before registration

Decisions the author makes before the OSF registration is filed that change or add to
a rule in `docs/design.md`. Section numbers refer to that document. The log closes when
the registration is filed. Departures from the registered design after filing go in
`docs/deviations.md`.

| 2026-11-18 | 1.2, 8.4, 12, 16 | The proposal's H2 (a reform narrows the within-district Black–White gap and the within-district Hispanic–White gap) is split into H2 (gap b) and H3 (gap c). The proposal's H3 (growth with years since reform) becomes H4, and its H4 (effect per $1,000 is positive) becomes H5. The Romano–Wolf adjustment stays across the three gaps (a), (b) and (c), so it now spans H1, H2 and H3. | The two racial gaps are separate outcomes with separate estimates and bound sets, and they can move in different directions. One hypothesis for both would have no single verdict when they disagree. The multiplicity family is unchanged because it was always defined over the three gaps. |

| 2026-11-18 | 12.3 | Verdict rule for H1, H2 and H3: supported when the bound set at M̄ = 1 lies wholly above zero and the agreement criterion holds; contradicted when it lies wholly below zero and the criterion holds; not supported otherwise; not evaluable when no bound set exists. | 2026-11-18 | 12.2 | Agreement criterion: each of the four secondary estimators gives an overall average of the same sign as the primary estimate inside the primary estimate's 95 percent bootstrap interval, and so does each registered sample and weighting variant that exists for the family. Models that cannot be estimated do not count either way; with fewer than three estimable secondary estimators the criterion is not evaluated. Two-way fixed effects, the narrow event sets and the timing checks are not part of it. | The proposal requires agreement "in sign and approximate size" across estimators and under the sample and weighting variants without a numerical test. The primary estimate's own bootstrap interval is a scale-free tolerance that does not break down near zero, as a ratio rule would. |
| 2026-11-18 | 12.3, 8.4 | H4 is evaluated with the growth contrast G, the mean of the estimated event-time effects +4 to +8 minus the mean of 0 to +3, with its bootstrap interval, a Romano–Wolf adjustment across the three gaps, and relative-magnitudes bound sets on the contrast. H4 is supported when the bound set of G at M̄ = 1 lies wholly above zero, the gap's overall effect is positive, and G has the same sign in each estimable secondary estimator. | The proposal states H4 but not a test of growth. Comparing the first four post-reform years with the later ones follows the four-year horizon of the spending literature and uses only the event-time estimates and draws the design already produces. |
| 2026-11-18 | 12.3, 9 | H5 is evaluated only where the first-stage revenue effect's 95 percent bootstrap interval lies wholly above zero. It is then supported when the dose-scaled interval lies wholly above zero. Otherwise H5 is not evaluable for that gap. The continuous-treatment estimates are reported beside the verdict and do not change it. | A per-$1,000 effect has a meaning only where the reform raised revenue. When the first stage includes zero the ratio's confidence set is unbounded, and when it is negative the ratio does not describe reform-induced revenue. The continuous estimator is exploratory under the proposal. |
