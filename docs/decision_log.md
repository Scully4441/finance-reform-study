# Author decisions before registration

Decisions the author made before the OSF registration is filed that change or
add to a rule in docs/design.md. Section numbers refer to that document.
Departures from the registered design after filing go in docs/deviations.md.

| Date | Section | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-11 | 5 (rule 3), 9 | Percent proficient reported as a range no wider than 10 percentage points enters at the range midpoint. Wider ranges and suppressed cells stay missing, and the 30-student floor stays. Width and midpoint come from the printed endpoints, with one-sided labels closed at 0 and 100 (20-29: width 9, midpoint 24.5; GE95: width 5, midpoint 97.5). Robustness samples: exact values only (the rule as written) and ranges of 5 points or less. This replaces "reported as an exact value (not a range) ... Range-reported cells are treated as missing and are not imputed" in rule 3 and "No imputation" in the Section 9 table. In the stage-1 files EDFacts reports high school percent proficient as a whole number only for groups of more than 300 tested students; groups of 31-60 get 10-point ranges and groups of 61-300 get 5-point ranges (`outputs/03_sample/range_widths_by_bracket.csv`). | The exact-only rule left about 200 district-years per subject-year for gaps (b) and (c), all in the largest districts. A range midpoint is measurement error in the outcome, bounded at half the range width and unrelated to treatment, which does not bias a difference-in-differences estimate and only widens its interval. The error is not classical: it depends on where the true rate falls within its band and is larger in probit units at the tails, so the no-bias claim is approximate; the exact-only and 5-point samples are the check. |
| 2026-09-11 | 5 | Districts that pass rules 1 and 2 but have no SAIPE 2009 child-poverty rate are dropped for all three gaps. Section 5 has no such rule. | The SAIPE 2009 child-poverty rate defines the fixed poverty quintiles for gap (a) and is a pre-treatment covariate in every model, so a district without it cannot be placed in a quintile or enter the doubly robust adjustment. The 199 districts are mostly agencies SAIPE does not cover (192 are absent from the SAIPE file; 7 have no children 5-17), 73 of them in the primary event set. |
| 2026-09-11 | 5 (rule 4) | The 95 percent participation test applies to the exact value where one is reported and to the band midpoint where participation is banded, with the same printed-endpoint convention as rule 3: GE90 (midpoint 95) passes; 90-94 (92), 80-89 and lower bands fail; nothing reported fails. This replaces the earlier reading of the same day, under which the band's lower bound had to reach 95 and GE90 failed. The exact-only robustness sample keeps exact participation values: its cells report participation as an exact value or GE99, and GE99 passes. | The same logic as the midpoint rule, and it reduces the 2012-to-2013 discontinuity in sample composition rather than removing it. Under the lower-bound reading, districts with 31-60 tested students, whose top participation band is GE90, could not pass from 2012-13 on. The effective threshold depends on district size because of the band widths: districts with 31 to 60 tested students pass on GE90, districts with 61 to 300 fail at 90-94, and only the largest districts face the literal 95. |
| 2026-09-11 | 6 (gap (a)), 7 | The enrollment weight in gap (a), the enrollment-weighted mean district score by poverty quintile, is the district's total membership in the 2009-10 CCD LEA universe file (`MEMBER`), fixed across years. Section 6 does not name the enrollment measure or its year. | The weight is pre-treatment, so the reform cannot move it, and it matches the 2009 enrollment covariate in Section 7. Same-year grade 9-12 membership or tested counts would let the reform change the weights. |
| 2026-09-11 | 6 (gaps (b), (c)) | V for the racial gaps is Black minus White and Hispanic minus White: V = Φ⁻¹(p_Black) − Φ⁻¹(p_White) and V = Φ⁻¹(p_Hispanic) − Φ⁻¹(p_White). Reardon's convention is the reverse (White minus Black). | Section 6 fixes gap (a) as the top-poverty quintile minus the bottom-poverty quintile, which is disadvantaged minus advantaged. Ordering (b) and (c) the same way makes all three gaps point in the same direction: gaps are usually negative, and a positive effect means the gap narrowed. |
| 2026-09-11 | 6 (gap (a)) | A district without a valid 2009-10 CCD membership (`MEMBER` coded -1, -2 or -9, or 0) is left out of gap (a) and stays in gaps (b) and (c). In stage 1 this affects four California quintile-5 districts whose 2009-10 `MEMBER` is -9 (fails NCES quality standards) and is valid in later years: 0609810, 0623160, 0626760 and 0691134. 0623160 has about 16,000 students. | The weight is fixed at 2009-10 and pre-treatment. Filling from 2010-11 would let a possibly post-treatment value in for early cohorts, and filling from SAIPE ages 5-17 would mix two measures. Like the SAIPE rule, a district that cannot be weighted cannot enter the weighted mean. |
| 2026-09-11 | 7 | The Callaway-Sant'Anna models (step 5) leave out the assessment-replacement flag and CEP availability, which Section 7 names as the time-varying controls. The flags stay available for the transition-year robustness exclusion (Section 9) and for the regression-based secondary estimators (step 6). | did::att_gt conditions only on baseline covariates and cannot take time-varying controls. Residualizing the outcome on the flags before estimation would be a procedure the design does not describe. |
| 2026-09-11 | 6, 7 | The outcome in estimation is the mean of math V and RLA V in a unit-year (district-year for gaps (b) and (c), state-year for gap (a)). Both subjects are required: if either is missing, the unit-year is missing. | Section 6 says the subjects are averaged within district-year but does not say what happens when one is missing. Requiring both keeps the outcome the same measure in every unit-year; a one-subject year would mix measures. |
| 2026-09-11 | 7 | Balanced panel: a district (gaps (b), (c)) or state (gap (a)) enters a Callaway-Sant'Anna model only with the outcome in every window year. Units dropped by this rule are counted in `outputs/05_primary/panel_counts.csv`. | This is did's standard panel estimator, and the estimate is then conditional on stable inclusion (Section 9). The unbalanced alternative switches did to its repeated cross-section form and lets the mix of districts change across years. The requirement gets stricter as stage 2 adds years. |
| 2026-09-11 | 7 | Covariates for gaps (b) and (c): log of 2009-10 CCD total membership (`MEMBER`, the gap (a) weight); the SAIPE 2009 child-poverty rate; and the Black and Hispanic shares of 2009-10 CCD school membership, summed within the district over schools reporting valid `BLACK`, `HISP` and `TOTETH` counts, all grades, as shares of `TOTETH`. A district without a valid value for any of them is left out of the gap (b) and (c) models. | Section 7 names the three characteristics but not their form. District size is heavily skewed, so in levels a few very large districts would dominate the propensity and outcome regressions. The Black and Hispanic shares cover the groups in gaps (b) and (c). Leaving out districts with a missing value follows the SAIPE rule. |
| 2026-09-11 | 7 (gap (a)) | Gap (a) is estimated with no covariates (`xformla = ~1`), so the doubly robust estimator reduces to unconditional difference-in-differences with not-yet-treated controls. | Gap (a) is a state-year outcome, and Section 7's covariates are district characteristics. With cohorts of one or two states, a state-level propensity model is close to perfect separation. |
| 2026-09-11 | 7 (weighting) | The tested-count weight for the gap (b) and (c) robustness models is the number of students in the gap's two groups (Black + White, or Hispanic + White) tested in 2009-10, averaged over math and RLA, fixed across years. It enters through did's `weightsname`. | Section 7 says the robustness model is weighted by the number of students tested in the two groups but does not name a year. The 2009-10 count is pre-treatment for every cohort that can be estimated, so the reform cannot move the weights, and it matches the fixed 2009-10 weight in gap (a). |
